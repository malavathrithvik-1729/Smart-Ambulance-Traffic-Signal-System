Smart Ambulance Traffic Signal System

A decentralized, hybrid software-and-hardware intelligent emergency response framework developed by students of SPR High School, Kamareddy. It coordinates the entire emergency journey from call receipt and dynamic route planning to real-time traffic signal pre-emption and trauma hospital handover.

📥 Open Original PDFs: 📄 View Problem & Solution PDF 📊 View Research Evidences PDF 📖 View Website Demo Manual PDF
⚠️

1. Problem

During an emergency, an ambulance can lose critical time at several stages of its journey—from receiving the emergency call and identifying the accident location to reaching the patient and transporting the patient to a suitable hospital. In rural and semi-urban areas, callers may describe locations using unclear landmarks, making the exact incident location difficult to identify.

🚨 Core Bottleneck: After dispatch, the ambulance may face unfamiliar roads, heavy traffic, traffic signals, railway crossings, blocked intersections, and vehicles that are unaware of the approaching emergency vehicle. Even after reaching the accident scene, the journey to the hospital can be delayed again because traffic conditions may have changed. A traffic signal turning green alone does not guarantee that the route ahead is clear.

Therefore, the problem is not only traffic-signal delay, but the lack of a coordinated system that can support the ambulance journey from emergency call to accident scene and from accident scene to hospital.
💡

2. Solution

We propose a hybrid software-and-hardware Smart Ambulance Traffic Signal System designed to support an ambulance throughout its complete emergency journey—from receiving the emergency call to reaching the accident scene and finally transporting the patient to a suitable hospital. Instead of focusing only on changing a traffic signal, the proposed system combines AI-assisted location identification, dynamic route planning, traffic prediction, V2X/RF communication, driver alerts, coordinated traffic-signal control, and hospital-route management.

System Capabilities Breakdown:
  • AI-Assisted Incident Location: Uses available location information, map data, and caller description to resolve landmarks into verified coordinates, minimizing call back delays.
  • Dynamic Route Planning & Traffic Prediction: Generates multiple feasible routes and selects the optimal path based on real-time traffic conditions and AI/ML predicted congestion.
  • Dynamic Emergency Corridor (Haversine Proximity): The ambulance broadcasts its position. Signals within 280 m turn green and reset to red 350 m past the intersection.
  • Acoustic & Visual Driver Alerts: Speakers at crowded intersections announce "Emergency ambulance approaching. Please give way safely."
  • Dual Journey Management: Coordinates Journey 1 (Base → Scene) and Journey 2 (Scene → Hospital) with continuous monitoring and dynamic re-routing.
  • First-Aid AI Assistant: Guides callers with CPR, hemorrhage control, and fracture immobilization protocols while the vehicle is in transit.

Proposed Technologies

Technology Proposed Use in the System
AI / Machine Learning Predict traffic conditions, assist incident-location identification, compare possible routes and support dynamic route selection.
GPS & GIS Mapping Track ambulance position, identify incident location, map routes and locate suitable hospitals.
Dynamic Route Planning Generate multiple possible routes and continuously select a suitable route based on current and predicted traffic.
RF Communication Low-cost prototype communication between the ambulance module and traffic-signal module.
V2X Communication Future communication between the ambulance, connected vehicles and roadside infrastructure.
Microcontroller Processes RF/V2X-related signals and controls the prototype traffic-signal system.
Traffic Sensors / Cameras Detect traffic queues, blocked intersections and changing road conditions.
ETA-Based Signal Coordination Estimate ambulance arrival time and prepare upcoming traffic signals before the ambulance reaches them.
Driver Alert System Warn nearby road users about approaching emergency vehicle and encourage safe yielding.
Hospital Information System Support selection of suitable and reachable hospital using emergency facilities, ICU availability and predicted travel time.
Real-Time Monitoring & Re-routing Continuously monitor emergency corridor and change route when unexpected congestion or blockage occurs.

3. Evidences & Existing Technologies

The need for an integrated emergency corridor is supported by peer-reviewed research, empirical field data, and government technical guidelines.

Evidence 1

Ambulance Journeys Contain Multiple Sources of Delay

A peer-reviewed Scientific Reports (Nature) study analyzed 38,823 emergency medical service records across all 36 districts of Maharashtra, India (November 2022).

Stage Average Time
Preparation3.53 min
Base → Scene23.15 min
Scene Assessment12.12 min
Scene → Hospital39.41 min
Hospital Handover10.85 min
Return41.89 min

Source: Scientific Reports — Nature (2023). Dataset: 38,823 EMS records.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-48713-1

Evidence 2

Ambulance Reachability & Trauma Transport Remain Challenging

A survey-based study of 225 participants investigating accidents on Indian National Highways revealed that successfully contacting an ambulance does not guarantee prompt arrival:

  • Successfully contacted ambulance service: ~90%
  • Ambulance reached accident scene: ~72–78%
  • Ambulance arrival time on core highways: ~25–35 min
  • Transport time to nearby hospital: ~15–25 min

Source: Road accidents on Indian National highways, ambulance reachability and transportation of injured to trauma facility: Survey-based introspection of golden hour (2024).
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38605810/

Evidence 3

Traffic Conditions Make Ambulance Response Times Highly Variable

A 2023 Delhi study ("Capturing delays in response of emergency services in Delhi") utilized a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) showing that travel time fluctuates widely throughout the day, proving that fixed route planning without dynamic prediction fails in congested environments.

Source: Socio-Economic Planning Sciences (2023), Wajid & Nizamuddin.
ScienceDirect Article Link

Evidence 4

India's National Ambulance Framework Supports GPS, GIS & Intelligent Dispatch

The Ministry of Health & Family Welfare, Government of India, in its Operational Guidelines on National Ambulance Services (NAS), 2026, officially mandates GPS tracking, digital call handling, GIS hospital mapping, and intelligent fleet dispatch.

Source: Ministry of Health & Family Welfare, Govt of India (2026).
PIB Press Release Link

Evidence 5

Singapore's Traffic Priority System (TPS) Targets the Final Mile

Developed by Singapore Civil Defence Force (SCDF), Land Transport Authority (LTA), and HTX. Implemented across 8 public hospitals with over 2,500 activations, saving up to 1 minute 57 seconds per casualty conveyance within ~200 m of hospital intersections.

Source: Singapore Civil Defence Force (SCDF) Annual Statistics (2025).

Evidence 6

US Systems Show How Signal Pre-emption Cuts Travel Time by 16–23%

Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) documentation of 22 intersections in Houston, Texas proved average emergency-vehicle travel time was reduced by 16% to 23% using coordinated pre-emption and V2I communication.

Source: FHWA, U.S. Department of Transportation.

Evidence 7

India Has Already Trialed V2X-Based Green Corridors

Telecommunications Engineering Centre (TEC), Government of India, documented successful V2X green corridor trials in Ahmedabad integrating Variable Message Signs (VMS) with traffic signals for automated pre-emption.

Source: TEC, Govt of India Technical Report on Intelligent Transport Systems.

Conclusion: Existing global and Indian systems prove that dynamic green corridors work. Team Lifeline integrates this technology with voice-based caller dispatch, AI first-aid assistance, and hospital readiness tracking into one accessible platform.
